A long time ago, in the green land of Hyrule, there lived two brothers named Aidan and Declan.

They lived in a little wooden house at the edge of Kakariko Village. Wind chimes hung by the door. Chickens wandered the yard, and every night a sleepy cucco named Biscuit settled onto the porch rail to keep watch.

Aidan was the brave one. He could climb the tallest tree in the village and knew every path through Lost Woods. Declan was the clever one. He could read the old Sheikah stones, solve any puzzle, and hear the difference between a happy bird and a worried one.

Together they were the heroes of Hyrule.

One evening the sky over Hyrule Field turned purple and gold. The brothers were sitting on the porch eating warm apple bread when Declan pointed up.

"Aidan, look!"

A tiny star was falling. It drifted down slowly, like a feather, and landed far away beyond the hills with a soft, silvery *ting*.

Then the lights in the sky began to flicker. One star went out, then another.

The village gate creaked open, and an old woman hurried up the path. It was Impa, the wisest person in Kakariko.

"Boys," she said, "that was the Star of Rest. Every night it hangs above Hyrule and sings a quiet song that helps the whole kingdom fall asleep. Without it, the owls can't settle, the fairies can't dream, and even Princess Zelda will lie awake all night. Someone has to bring it home before the moon is high."

Aidan stood up straight. "We'll go."

Declan nodded. "We'll find it."

They grabbed their things. Aidan took his wooden shield and a lantern. Declan took his map and a little blue fairy named Pip, who glowed whenever danger was near.

They ran across Hyrule Field while the grass whispered around their ankles. Pip zipped ahead, lighting the way.

Soon they reached a river, wide and dark and rushing fast. There was no bridge.

"I can't jump that far," Aidan said.

Declan looked at the water and at the big flat stones poking out of it, then back at his map. "The stones make a pattern. Three steps left, two steps right, then one big hop. It's a puzzle."

Aidan grinned. "Then you call out the steps, and I'll jump."

*Left, left, left. Right, right. HOP.*

They landed safely on the other side, laughing.

Next they came to the edge of the Lost Woods. The trees were tall and twisty, and the path split in every direction. A spooky echo floated through the leaves.

Pip began to glow bright blue.

"Something's in there," Declan whispered.

Out of the shadows waddled a little Deku Scrub with leaves sticking out of his head. He was crying.

"Waaah! I can't sleep! The star stopped singing, and now I'm lost and tired and grumpy!"

Aidan knelt down beside him. "We're looking for the star too. Want to come with us?"

The Deku Scrub sniffled. "You'd let me?"

"Heroes help everybody," said Aidan.

The scrub knew the forest's secret: listen for the song. The three of them stood very still and very quiet, and from deep in the woods they heard the faintest tune, soft as a lullaby. *La-la-la-laaa.*

They followed it through the trees, under an arch of roots, and into a hidden meadow full of glowing flowers.

There in the middle, nestled in the moss, sat the Star of Rest. It was smaller than a peach and shimmering, but its song was faint and tired.

"It fell because it got sleepy," Declan said gently. "It sings for everyone else, but nobody ever sings to it."

Aidan looked at his brother. "Then let's sing to it."

So they sat down in the moss: Aidan, Declan, Pip, and the little Deku Scrub. They hummed the quietest, gentlest song they knew, the one their mother used to sing to them on rainy nights.

The star began to glow brighter, and brighter, and brighter still.

Then it rose up out of the moss, light as a bubble, and floated past the treetops back into the sky. It settled into its place and began to sing.

All across Hyrule, the owls tucked their heads under their wings. The fairies curled up inside flowers. The horses at Lon Lon Ranch closed their eyes. Far away in Hyrule Castle, Princess Zelda gave a big yawn and smiled.

The Deku Scrub was already snoring in the moss.

Aidan and Declan walked home together under the stars with Pip glowing softly between them. Biscuit the cucco was waiting on the porch rail, and their beds were warm.

"We did it," whispered Declan.

"We always do," whispered Aidan. "Because we do it together."

Above them, the Star of Rest sang its quiet song, and the two heroes of Hyrule fell fast asleep.

The End.
